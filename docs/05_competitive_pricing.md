# Competitive Pricing

Reference data for the thresholds in [`01_hypotheses.md`](01_hypotheses.md) (H7). Prices change;
every row carries a source and an access date. Rows without both are not usable.

---

## 1. Method

- Prices are **gross, consumer-facing**, as displayed to an EU buyer, in EUR.
- Where a brand operates regional storefronts, the EU store is used unless stated
  otherwise.
- **Entry price** = lowest non-sale price for a standard product in the range.
- **Benchmarks use list price only.** Data was collected in August, in the middle
  of a broad seasonal discounting period, and several brands were running
  simultaneous promotions. Sale prices observed in this window describe the
  season, not the category's normal price level. They are recorded with dates as
  observations and are excluded from every derived reference value.
- Where a brand's entire range appears permanently discounted, that is noted for
  re-checking outside the seasonal window rather than treated as the real price.
- Accessories, bundles, and collaboration editions are excluded from entry-price
  calculation.

---

## 2. Category taxonomy

The three types below are routinely conflated in reviews and search results. Only
the first is a direct competitor.

| Type | Description | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Hip sling / carry assist** | Single shoulder strap, deep fabric seat, unstructured, packs flat. Assisted carry — one arm remains around the child. | Wildride, Ergobaby Upsie |
| **Hip seat** | Waist belt with a rigid or foam seat shelf, no shoulder strap. Assisted carry. | Ergobaby Lift, Ergobaby Alta, Tushbaby |
| **Soft structured carrier** | Two shoulder straps plus waist belt, hands-free. | Ergobaby Omni, BabyBjörn, Boba |

"Carry assist" is the term the trade press has begun using for the first
category. Worth carrying into keyword research alongside "hip sling", since
search volume may split across both.

| Field | Value |
|---|---|

| Positioning | Ergonomic compact two-position sling; inward facing, front and hip carry |
| Free shipping threshold | EUR 55 |

**This is the lowest list price in the direct set, from the largest brand in it.**
Ergobaby's own toddler range is priced above it — Lift hip seat at EUR 79,90 and
Alta hip seat at EUR 129,90 — so the sling sits at the entry of their ladder
rather than at its top.

The product occupies the differentiator claimed in
[`01_hypotheses.md`](01_hypotheses.md). Its strap construction should be
examined directly rather than from marketing copy before positioning is
finalised.

Store locale: Netherlands (EUR)

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Origin | Netherlands |
| SKUs in range | 33 |
| **Entry price** | **EUR 79** |
| Mid tier | EUR 89 |
| Premium tier | EUR 149 |
| Highest price in collection | EUR 349 |
| Free EU shipping threshold | EUR 90 |
| Age / weight | 9 months – 4 years, max 20 kg |
| Stated conformity | ASTM F2907-22; CEN/TR 16512-2015 |

**Tier composition.** The ladder is driven by fabric and print, not by features:
plain cotton at entry, linen and animal prints in the middle, graphic and
patterned at the top. Function appears constant across tiers, so the category
monetises aesthetics rather than ergonomics. Worth weighing against the intended
differentiator.

**Discounting observed 2026-07-30**, roughly 40–47% and concentrated in the
EUR 99–129 range. Seasonal window; not used as a benchmark. Worth re-checking
outside August to see whether the concentration in the upper-middle tier
persists, which would suggest weaker sell-through there rather than a seasonal
promotion.

**Note on conformity.** Wildride does not cite EN 13209-2. If CEN/TR 16512 is the
applicable reference for slings, the certification path assumed in
[`01_hypotheses.md`](01_hypotheses.md) §5 may be wrong, and with it the cost
estimate. To be confirmed with a testing laboratory or notified body.


### Minimonkey — Toddler Carrier (6m+)

Source: `https://www.minimonkey.com/en/toddler-carrier/`
Store locale: Netherlands (EUR)

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Origin | Netherlands |
| **List price** | **EUR 140** |
| Age / weight | 6 months – 20 kg |
| Weight | 0,58 kg |
| Material | 100% recycled mesh; waterproof, quick-drying |
| Features | Magnetic buckles; head cover doubling as pack pouch; front and back carry |
| Free shipping threshold | EUR 170 international, EUR 35 NL |
| Stated conformity | "conform EU safety standard"; no standard number given |

**Category caveat.** This is not a single-strap hip sling. It has shoulder straps
and a waistband, attaches at either the front panel or the belt, and supports
back carry — closer to a compact structured carrier than to the sling set. It is
recorded here because it competes for the same occasion (quick ups and downs with
a toddler, packs small) rather than because it shares the form factor.

**Relevant as a price ceiling.** At EUR 140 it demonstrates that the occasion
supports a price roughly double the sling entry point, but it is paired with
technical material claims and a distinct mechanism. It is evidence that features
can be monetised in this space, not that a sling can be priced there.

Conformity is asserted without naming a standard, which is worth noting alongside
Wildride's explicit citations when the certification path is decided.

### Tushbaby — Liftoff Toddler Sling

Source: `https://tushbaby.com/products/liftoff-toddler-sling`
Store locale: **United States (USD)**

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Origin | United States |
| **List price** | **USD 92** |
| Sale price observed 2026-07-30 | USD 80, back-to-school promotion (not used as benchmark) |
| Age / weight | 18+ months, 25–55 lb |
| Material | 100% polyester, padded non-slip seat |
| Warranty | 12 months; 60-day risk-free trial |
| Free shipping threshold | USD 60, contiguous US only |
| Reviews at access | 96, averaging 4.27 / 5 |

**US store; duties and taxes on EU orders are paid by the customer.** The
landed EU cost is therefore materially above the listed price, and this is not a
like-for-like comparison with the EU-priced entries above. Whether a European
storefront exists should be checked before treating it as a direct EU
competitor.

**Two features of this product bear directly on the project.**

*The shoulder claim is already made here.* The product is described as a
one-shoulder design with a split padded mesh shoulder intended to improve weight
distribution — the same problem the intended differentiator addresses. Combined
with Ergobaby Upsie, this means the shoulder-ergonomics position is occupied by
two established brands rather than being open.

*An accessory ecosystem is already built around the sling.* It ships with an
integrated phone pocket, a tuckaway bottle holder, and D-rings for clipping the
brand's own accessories. Those accessories are sold separately across a range of
roughly USD 11 to USD 63. This is a demonstrated attach-rate model in exactly
this category, not a hypothetical one.

**Reviews are the most useful asset here.** With 96 reviews averaging 4.27, the
distribution is wide enough to contain substantive criticism, and several themes
recur: shoulder strain despite the padded strap, fit problems for larger-bodied
parents, difficulty adjusting strap length while the child is seated, a pocket
that cannot be repositioned, and at least one stitching failure. These are
revealed pain points in the exact product category, and they cost nothing to
collect. Recorded here as a pointer; the systematic review analysis belongs to
the triangulation step in [`01_hypotheses.md`](01_hypotheses.md).

If Serbia remains a launch market, domestic brands need pricing and category
classification. Note that Nest For Rest already ships to Serbia in EUR.

Store locale: Serbia (EUR)

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| SKUs in range | 6 |
| **List price** | **EUR 79 – 89** |

| Age / weight | 9 months – 4 years, max 20 kg |
| Self-description | Premium toddler carrier |
| Stated conformity | not stated on the collection page |

**The entire range was discounted at access**, at a site-wide 25%. This falls
inside the seasonal window, so the list range of EUR 79–89 is what enters the
benchmark. That range matches Wildride's entry and mid tiers.

One caveat worth re-checking outside August: a site-wide discount covering every
SKU can indicate a permanent reference-price strategy rather than a promotion. If
the same discount is still running in, say, October, the list price is decorative
and the benchmark should move. Two of six SKUs were out of stock at access.

**Ships to Serbia in EUR**, which is directly relevant if Serbia remains a launch
market — the domestic field is not limited to domestic brands.

### Revision of the entry price

The entry price was initially recorded as EUR 79, from Wildride, when Wildride
was the only direct competitor priced. Adding Ergobaby lowers it to **EUR 59,90**.
The revision is recorded rather than made silently, because the qualifying WTP
threshold in [`01_hypotheses.md`](01_hypotheses.md) was originally justified by
reference to the EUR 79 figure.

### Why the qualifying band is unchanged

The threshold stays at `80-110`, but the justification is now stronger rather
than weaker.

The survey band `50-80` was originally set aside because it *contained* the entry
price. With the fuller set it turns out to contain **most of the EU direct set's
list prices** — Upsie at EUR 59,90, Wildride's entry tier at EUR 79, and the lower
end of Nest For Rest. A respondent selecting that band is stating a willingness
to pay roughly what the category already charges, which is compatible with buying
an existing product and says little about a new entrant.

The band `80-110` sits above every EU list price except Wildride's mid tier and
above. It is a demanding threshold, and deliberately so: it identifies
respondents willing to pay at or above the top of the established range, which is
the only group a new brand without recognition can be priced against.

This makes the qualified intent metric conservative. That is stated in the
findings rather than corrected for.

### Standing implication for pricing

Nothing here fixes a target price — that is set from the floor in
[`01_hypotheses.md`](01_hypotheses.md). But the direct set now brackets the
question: the category's largest brand lists a sling at EUR 59,90, the
category-defining brand starts at EUR 79, and a differentiated compact carrier
reaches EUR 140. A new entrant priced near the bottom of that range competes on
price against brands with distribution and recognition; priced near the top, it
must carry a claim the buyer can perceive before purchase.
